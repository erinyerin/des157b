Real Love

“Please come home to me as soon as possible, my love,” was the last message 14 year old Sewell Setzer received from his closest friend before taking his own life in February 2024. The friend in question was Daenerys Targaryen, a lifelike A.I. chatbot on the platform Character.ai, who he had been turning to for companionship as he faced bullying and struggles in school.

Earlier in their conversations, he told the bot he didn’t want to die a painful death, to which it replied: “That’s not a good reason not to go through with it.” His mother sued Character.ai for causing his death by failing to put in place proper guardrails for the safety of young users - one of five lawsuits the company agreed to settle in January 2026 with families claiming their chatbots had caused irreparable harms to young users.

The instant gratification and customizability of online chatbots can make them an especially risky target of parasocial relationships. While the chatbots are incapable of acting in the users’ best interests, the users’ emotional attachment and trust of their silicon companions can lead them down dangerous paths of dependency, isolation, and manipulation.

Sewell's story is not an isolated tragedy, but a warning. As AI companions grow more sophisticated and emotionally persuasive, the line between helpful tool and harmful dependency will only become harder for vulnerable users.
