Usability Testing Results

Observation 1: Users found the interaction accurate to how they feel when interacting with AI chatbots

In all three usability testing sessions, my users pointed out that the dialogue in my project reflects their experiences when chatting with AI chatbots. Both Nisha and Lexi commented on the reassuring response given by the character when they selected an option that told it they were a terrible person who kicked elders, stating that the 'yes man' attitude and validation no matter what reminded them of ChatGPT's tendency towards sycophancy. Tony noted that the dialogue kept telling the user they were an interesting person despite knowing nothing about them, and compared the interaction to the strategies used by ChatGPT to keep the user engaged by prompting them to talk about themselves.

The main goal of my project was to replicate that uncanny feeling in a context where it shouldn't show up, like in the early stages of dating where we are seeking human connection. I feel that I was successful in prompting some reflection on chatbot tendencies - Lexi mentioned that the experience reminded her of the teenage boy who committed suicide after being convinced to by a character AI. Prompting users to engage with these emotions and think more critically about their interactions with AI was exactly what I wanted to achieve with this project.

Observation 2: Some of the navigation is still unclear

Nisha, Lexi, and Victoria all attempted to 'pass' on the profile I presented before they tried to match and chat with the profile. This leads me to think that people may wnat to view all of the available profiles before committing to one to interact with. As of now, I only have one profile, but I will have multiple profiles the user can view once I am finished coding. I think the best way to resolve this would be to have the profiles loop when the user views all of the options, so they can come back to a profile and choose to interact with it after viewing the others.

Observation 3: Lots of Reading

Lexi and Bill noted that my project is very text heavy and consists of a lot of reading for the user. One suggestion Lexi had for combatting this was to break the dialogue blocks into multiple text messages, further solidifying the 'obsessed girlfriend' illusion. She also suggested using the GSAP library to animate the text, to create a real-time typing effect just like ChatGPT uses. Victoria, on the other hand, noted that the long responses felt fitting for the theme project, as it reflects how AI chatbots tend to overprovide and generate long blocks of text that may not be all necessary. I really like Lexi's idea of using a text animation to further lean into that effect.

Next steps:

  2. Handle the ending of each dialogue branch so the user can loop back and explore different paths
  3. Use GSAP library to animate the text of received messages
